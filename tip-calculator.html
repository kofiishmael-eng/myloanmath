Common tip percentages

15% is generally considered the minimum for acceptable sit-down restaurant service in the US, 18-20% is standard for good service, and 20-25% is customary for exceptional service. These norms vary meaningfully by region and by type of service — delivery drivers, bartenders, and salon services often follow different conventions than a full-service restaurant.

Pre-tax or post-tax?

Most etiquette guides recommend tipping on the pre-tax subtotal, not the total after sales tax is added — tax isn't part of what the server or service provider earns, so tipping on it doesn't actually benefit them and just inflates the bill unnecessarily. That said, plenty of people simply tip on the final total out of convenience, and both are common in practice. Enter whichever amount you intend to base the tip on.

What to actually tip, and on what

In the United States, 15% is generally understood as the floor for adequate sit-down restaurant service, 18–20% as standard, and above 20% as genuinely good. The figure has drifted upward over time, which is why older advice suggesting 10–15% now reads as low. Other service types run on different conventions: counter service and takeaway carry no expectation, delivery is commonly tipped at a similar rate to dining in with a floor of a few dollars on small orders, and taxi or rideshare journeys usually attract 10–15%.

Pre-tax or post-tax? The traditional convention is to tip on the pre-tax subtotal, on the reasoning that sales tax is money the restaurant never sees. In practice most people tip on the total because it is the number printed largest, and on a $60 bill the difference is roughly a dollar. Neither choice is wrong; the calculator above works from whatever figure you enter, so enter the subtotal if you want the traditional calculation.

Service charges, and when not to tip twice

Check the bill before adding anything. Large parties are frequently subject to an automatic gratuity, often 18–20%, which is a mandatory charge rather than a tip and will be printed as a separate line. Some venues also apply a service charge that may or may not reach staff. Adding a further tip on top of an automatic gratuity is common and entirely optional; the one thing worth avoiding is doing it by accident because the line item was easy to miss.

A related trap on card terminals: some prompt for a tip calculated on the post-service-charge total, which quietly compounds one percentage on top of another.

Tipping outside the United States

American norms travel badly. Across much of Europe a service charge is usually included and rounding up or leaving a small amount is the convention rather than a percentage. In Japan tipping is not customary and can cause genuine awkwardness. In Australia and New Zealand it is appreciated but not expected, because minimum wages are structured differently. The general rule when travelling is that a tipping norm reflects how staff are paid rather than how good the service is, so it is worth checking the local expectation rather than exporting a percentage.

Splitting the bill without shortchanging anyone

Splitting evenly is simplest and is what the people-count above does. It works well when everyone ordered comparably, and poorly when one person had a set menu and another had tap water. The alternative — splitting by item, then apportioning tax and tip proportionally — is fairer but slower, and the honest arithmetic is that the tip should scale with each person's share of the subtotal, not be divided equally on top of an unequal split.

One practical note on rounding: if you round the per-person amount down, the total collected falls short of the intended tip. Rounding each share up by a small amount and letting the excess land on the tip is the version that does not leave the server short.

Frequently asked questions

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax bill amount?

Convention varies, but tipping on the pre-tax subtotal is the more common practice and what most etiquette guides recommend. Enter whichever amount you intend to tip on.

What's a standard tip percentage?

15-20% is standard for sit-down restaurant service in the US, with 18-20% increasingly common. Delivery, bars, and other services often follow different norms.

Should I tip on the pre-tax or post-tax amount?

The traditional convention is pre-tax, since sales tax never reaches the restaurant. Most people use the total because it is the more prominent number, and on a typical bill the difference is around a dollar. Enter whichever figure you prefer above.

What if the bill already includes a service charge?

An automatic gratuity or service charge is a mandatory line item rather than a tip. Any further tip is entirely optional. Check the bill before adding one, since these lines are easy to miss on a card terminal.
