What actually comes out of a paycheck

Your salary and your take-home pay are two very different numbers. A $65,000 salary doesn't arrive as $65,000 spread evenly across your paychecks — federal income tax, FICA payroll taxes (Social Security and Medicare), and state income tax if your state has one, all come out first. This calculator subtracts all three to show the number that actually lands in your account each pay period.

Biweekly vs. semi-monthly: not the same thing

These two get confused constantly because they sound alike. Biweekly means every two weeks, which works out to 26 paychecks a year — and twice a year, you'll notice a month with three paychecks instead of two. Semi-monthly means twice a month, typically on fixed dates like the 1st and 15th, which is exactly 24 paychecks a year, always two per month, never three. For the same annual salary, biweekly paychecks are slightly smaller than semi-monthly ones, since the same total is split across more pay periods.

Why FICA doesn't change with your state

Federal payroll taxes — Social Security and Medicare — are the same percentage no matter where you live in the US, which is different from income tax. Social Security is withheld at a flat rate up to an annual wage cap, after which it stops being withheld for the rest of the year; Medicare has no cap but adds an extra rate above a high income threshold. Because these are federal programs, moving to a no-income-tax state like Texas or Florida doesn't reduce your FICA withholding at all — it only removes the state income tax portion, which this calculator shows separately so you can see exactly how much that specific piece is worth.

Why two people with the same salary can take home different amounts

Filing status changes your federal tax bracket and standard deduction, which changes your federal withholding even at an identical salary. State matters even more — someone earning $65,000 in a no-income-tax state keeps meaningfully more of their paycheck than the same salary in a state with a high income tax rate, purely because of where they live. This is exactly why the calculator asks for both: salary alone doesn't determine take-home pay, the combination of salary, filing status, and state does.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my paycheck less than salary divided by pay periods?

Federal tax, state tax (if your state has one), and FICA payroll taxes (Social Security and Medicare) all come out before you see the money — this calculator subtracts all three to show your real take-home pay per paycheck.

What's the difference between biweekly and semi-monthly pay?

Biweekly means every two weeks — 26 paychecks a year. Semi-monthly means twice a month, typically the 1st and 15th — 24 paychecks a year. They sound similar but produce a genuinely different per-paycheck amount for the same salary.

Does this include 401(k) or health insurance deductions?

Not yet — this shows take-home pay after federal tax, state tax, and FICA only. Pre-tax deductions like 401(k) contributions or health insurance premiums would reduce your paycheck further and aren't factored in here.

Does moving to a no-income-tax state eliminate all paycheck deductions?

No — federal tax and FICA (Social Security and Medicare) still apply everywhere in the US regardless of state. A no-income-tax state only removes the state tax portion of your withholding, not the federal portion.
