The exact figure

1 yard = 0.9144 m exactly — and 1 m = 1.09361329834 yards

The yard is the base unit of the whole imperial length system: a foot is a third of a yard, an inch a thirty-sixth, a mile 1,760 yards. When the 1959 agreement fixed the yard at exactly 0.9144 m, every other imperial length became exactly defined as a consequence.

A metre is about 9.4% longer than a yard, or roughly 3 inches. That closeness is what makes the pair distinctive: for rough purposes they are nearly interchangeable, and that is precisely what makes the errors hard to spot.

Sport is where the difference is visible

An American football field is 100 yards between goal lines, which is 91.44 m. A football pitch is typically 100 to 110 m, so the American field is noticeably shorter despite both being described as "a hundred".

Athletics went metric decades ago, which is why the 100 m sprint replaced the 100 yard dash and why old records are not directly comparable. A 100 yard time is inherently faster than a 100 m time because the distance is 8.56 m shorter. Swimming maintains both, and pool records are recorded separately for 25 m, 50 m and 25 yard pools, since the number of turns changes the times.

Where yards persist

Fabric is sold by the yard in the United States and by the metre nearly everywhere else, which matters when ordering material from abroad: a metre gives you about 9% more cloth than a yard, so ordering the same number of units gets you extra rather than short. Golf uses yards for course distances in the US and UK and metres in most other countries, so the same hole carries two different numbers.

Concrete is sold by the cubic yard in the US and the cubic metre elsewhere, and the cubed difference is substantial: a cubic metre is about 31% more than a cubic yard. Ordering concrete in the wrong unit is a much larger error than the linear 9% suggests, because the mistake compounds across three dimensions.

Why closeness is dangerous

With most conversions a directional error is obvious. Confusing miles and kilometres produces a number 60% off; confusing pounds and kilograms produces one more than twice as large. Either mistake announces itself.

Yards and metres differ by 9%, which frequently looks plausible. A plan calling for 50 metres of material where 50 yards was intended leaves you 4.6 m short — enough to matter, not enough to notice until you run out. The reliable defence is to convert explicitly rather than treating the units as equivalent, even when the difference feels too small to bother with.

Frequently asked questions

How many metres is 100 yards?

91.44 m exactly, since a yard is exactly 0.9144 m. An American football field is therefore about 8.5 m shorter than a 100 m track.

Is a metre longer than a yard?

Yes, by about 9.4%, or roughly three inches. That closeness makes the two easy to confuse and the resulting errors easy to miss.

Does the difference matter when ordering fabric?

Yes. A metre gives about 9% more cloth than a yard, so ordering metres when you meant yards leaves you with extra, and the reverse leaves you short.
