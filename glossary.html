Financial Glossary

Plain-English definitions for every term our calculators use — no jargon left unexplained.

Loans & Debt Tax & Paychecks Savings & Interest General Terms

Loans & Debt

APR (Annual Percentage Rate)

The interest rate on a loan plus most of its mandatory fees, expressed as one annualized percentage — meant to show the loan's true total cost, not just the rate used to calculate your monthly payment.

Read the full explanation →

Amortization

The process of paying off a loan through regular fixed payments, where each payment covers that period's interest first, with the remainder reducing the principal. Early payments are mostly interest; later payments are mostly principal.

Read the full explanation →

Principal

The original amount borrowed (or still owed), not counting interest. Your monthly payment is calculated from the principal, the interest rate, and the loan term.

Loan Calculator →

Interest Rate

The percentage a lender charges annually for the money you've borrowed. This is the number that directly determines your monthly payment, distinct from APR, which also folds in fees.

Loan Calculator →

Loan Term

How long you have to repay a loan, usually expressed in months or years. A longer term lowers your monthly payment but increases total interest paid over the life of the loan.

Down Payment

The upfront amount you pay toward a purchase before financing the rest. A larger down payment reduces the amount you finance, lowering both your monthly payment and total interest.

Mortgage Calculator →

Trade-In Value

The amount a dealer credits you for an existing vehicle when financing a new one, subtracted from the purchase price before calculating the loan amount.

Auto Loan Calculator →

PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)

Insurance a mortgage lender typically requires when your down payment is less than 20% of the home's price, protecting the lender (not you) if you default. It's added to your monthly payment and can usually be removed once you reach 20% equity.

Mortgage Calculator →

Minimum Payment

The smallest amount a credit card issuer requires you to pay each month, typically around 2% of your balance or a small flat floor like $25, whichever is greater. Paying only the minimum can take years longer and cost far more in interest than a larger fixed payment.

Debt Payoff Calculator →

Negative Equity ("Upside Down")

When you owe more on a loan than the asset (typically a car) is currently worth — common early in a car loan, since vehicles depreciate faster than a long loan term pays down the balance.

Auto Loan Calculator →

Tax & Paychecks

Marginal Tax Rate

The tax rate applied to the last dollar you earn — the top bracket your income reaches. It does not apply to your entire income, only the portion within that top bracket.

Read the full explanation →

Effective Tax Rate

Your total tax bill divided by your total income — the real, blended average rate you actually pay, which is always lower than your marginal rate.

Read the full explanation →

Standard Deduction

A fixed dollar amount the IRS lets you subtract from your income before calculating tax, without needing to itemize individual expenses. Most filers use this rather than itemizing.

US Tax Calculator →

Filing Status

A category (Single, Married Filing Jointly, Head of Household, Married Filing Separately) that determines which tax brackets and standard deduction apply to you.

US Tax Calculator →

FICA

The combined US payroll tax for Social Security and Medicare, withheld automatically from every paycheck. It's a flat percentage (with a cap on the Social Security portion), separate from federal and state income tax.

Salary Calculator →

CPP & EI

Canada Pension Plan and Employment Insurance — the Canadian equivalent of FICA, deducted automatically from every paycheque alongside federal and provincial income tax.

Canada Tax Calculator →

Basic Personal Amount (BPA)

The amount of income a Canadian resident can earn before paying any federal income tax — functionally similar to the US standard deduction, though structured as a tax credit rather than a deduction.

Canada Tax Calculator →

Gross Income vs. Net Income

Gross income is what you earn before any deductions. Net income (take-home pay) is what's left after income tax, payroll tax, and any other withholdings — the amount that actually reaches your bank account.

Salary Calculator →

Biweekly vs. Semi-Monthly

Biweekly means paid every two weeks (26 paychecks a year, with two three-paycheck months annually). Semi-monthly means paid twice a month on fixed dates like the 1st and 15th (24 paychecks a year, always two per month).

Salary Calculator →

Savings & Interest

Compound Interest

Interest calculated on both your original balance and any interest already earned — meaning your money earns interest on its own interest over time, growing faster the longer it's left to compound.

Read the full explanation →

Simple Interest

Interest calculated only on the original principal, every period, with no growth from previously earned interest. This is the mathematical opposite of compound interest.

Read the full explanation →

Future Value

What a sum of money today (or a series of contributions) will be worth at a specified point in the future, after accounting for interest or investment growth.

Compound Interest Calculator →

Compounding Frequency

How often interest is calculated and added to a balance — annually, monthly, or daily. More frequent compounding produces a slightly higher effective return on the same stated rate.

Compound Interest Calculator →

General Terms

Sales Tax

A percentage-based tax added to the price of goods at the point of sale, set by state, provincial, or local governments and varying widely by location.

Auto Loan Calculator →

Discount Rate (Percentage Off)

The percentage subtracted from an original price to reach a sale price. Two discounts applied one after another do not simply add together — the second discount applies to the already-reduced price.

Discount Calculator →

Percentage Change

How much a value has increased or decreased, expressed as a percentage of its original value — calculated as the difference divided by the original amount.

Percentage Calculator →
