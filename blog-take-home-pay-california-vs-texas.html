Take-Home Pay: California vs. Texas

Published 2026-07-31

"How much more would I actually take home in Texas?" is one of the most common questions behind a relocation or remote-work decision, and it has a precise answer — because federal tax and FICA are identical in every state, the entire gap between California and Texas take-home pay comes down to exactly one number: California's state income tax. Here's what that gap actually looks like at three real salary levels, computed the same way our salary calculator does it.

The pattern worth noticing

The gap doesn't stay flat as income rises — it grows, because California's tax is progressive while Texas's is a flat zero. At $75,000 the difference is about $144 a month; at $150,000 it's nearly $700 a month, roughly five times larger even though the salary only doubled. For higher earners specifically, the state-tax gap between a state like California and a no-income-tax state compounds meaningfully faster than income does.

What this comparison doesn't include

State income tax is one factor, not the whole cost-of-living picture. Housing costs, sales tax rates, property tax rates, and overall cost of living all vary independently of income tax and can easily outweigh the income tax difference in either direction — a lower state tax bill doesn't automatically mean more money left over at the end of the month once rent or a mortgage payment is accounted for. This comparison isolates income tax specifically because that's the piece with a precise, calculable answer; the rest depends heavily on where exactly in each state someone lives.

Run your own numbers

These figures use a single filer with no state-specific deductions beyond the standard federal one. Your real numbers will shift with filing status, deductions, and your actual salary — run your own scenario through the salary calculator for California, Texas, or any of the other states now covered, to see your specific take-home difference rather than these three reference points.
