Take-Home Pay: Oregon vs. Washington

By Ishmael, accountant · Published 2026-08-02

Portland and Vancouver sit ten minutes apart across the Columbia River, in two states with opposite tax structures. Oregon has one of the highest state income tax burdens on middle incomes in the country; Washington has none. Here is what that looks like at four salary levels, computed exactly the way our salary calculator does it.

The pattern worth noticing

This is the widest gap of any pair here at every salary level, and it is wide from the very bottom — Oregon’s brackets reach 8.75% at income levels where most states are still in low single digits. The offsetting fact every local knows: Oregon has no sales tax and Washington’s is high, so shopping habits genuinely claw some of this back. At $75,000 the difference is about $402.90 a month; at $250,000 it is $1,783.30 a month.

What this comparison doesn’t include

Two local factors cut hard against these figures. Residents of the Portland area pay additional local income taxes on top of Oregon state tax, and this calculator models state-level tax only — so a Portland resident's real gap against Washington is wider than shown. Pulling the other way, Oregon has no sales tax at all while Washington's is among the highest, which is exactly why cross-river shopping is a local habit rather than a curiosity.

A note on precision: these figures apply each state’s bracket schedule to your federal taxable income. Washington levies no personal income tax on wages, so its column is federal tax and FICA only. Oregon’s figure comes from its own published brackets. States also have their own credits, exemptions and deductions that this model does not carry, so treat the numbers as a close estimate of the gap rather than a filled-in tax return. The methodology page sets out exactly what is and is not modelled.

Run your own numbers

Every figure above assumes a single filer taking the standard federal deduction and nothing else. Your own result will move with filing status, deductions, and your actual salary — put your real number into the salary calculator for Oregon, Washington, or any other state, and you can share or bookmark the result straight from the page.
