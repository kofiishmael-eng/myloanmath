The exact figure, and why it is exact

1 lb = 0.45359237 kg exactly — and therefore 1 kg = 2.20462262185 lbs to eleven decimal places.

That first number is not the result of a measurement. It is a definition, agreed in 1959 by the national standards bodies of the United States, the United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, New Zealand and South Africa, which unified several slightly different pounds then in use into one international avoirdupois pound. Before that agreement the US pound and the British pound differed in the seventh decimal place, which mattered for trade in precious metals and pharmaceuticals and almost nothing else.

The practical consequence is that this conversion has no error bar. Any imprecision in a converted figure comes from rounding the answer, never from uncertainty in the factor.

The 2.2 shortcut, and where it breaks

Most people convert by multiplying by 2.2. That is a reasonable mental shortcut, but it is low by about 0.21%, and the error scales with the number. At 70 kg the shortcut gives 154 lbs against a true 154.32 — a third of a pound, invisible on a bathroom scale. At 500 kg it is short by more than a full kilogram's worth of pounds, and on a shipping manifest of several tonnes the discrepancy becomes a billing dispute.

If you want a better mental method, multiply by 2 and add 10%: 70 × 2 = 140, plus 14, gives 154. That is the same 2.2 in disguise. For real accuracy the only reliable approach is the full factor, which is what the tool above uses.

Pounds, mass, and the thing that trips up physics students

A pound is a unit of mass in the avoirdupois system used here, which is why a direct conversion to kilograms is meaningful at all. But "pound" is also used as a unit of force — pound-force, the weight of one pound-mass under standard gravity. In everyday use the distinction never surfaces, because scales are calibrated for Earth's surface and the two numbers coincide.

It matters in engineering, and it matters conceptually: your mass is identical on the Moon while your weight is about a sixth. A kilogram is unambiguously mass, which is one reason the metric system is easier to reason with in physics even when the imperial figure is more familiar.

Where each unit is still standard

Kilograms are the legal standard for trade almost everywhere, including in the UK, where goods must be sold by metric weight even though people continue to think in pounds and stones. The United States is the significant exception for retail. Aviation and shipping use kilograms globally regardless of the country, which is why baggage allowances are quoted in kg even on domestic US routes operated by international carriers.

For body weight specifically, the UK and Ireland use stone and pounds, the US uses pounds alone, and most of the rest of the world uses kilograms — three different conventions for the same measurement.

Frequently asked questions

How many pounds are in a kilogram?

Exactly 2.20462262185 pounds, derived from the definition 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg. The common shortcut of 2.2 is low by about 0.21%, which is negligible for body weight and significant for freight.

Is the kg to lbs conversion exact?

Yes. The factor is a definition set by the 1959 International Yard and Pound Agreement, not a measurement, so it has no uncertainty. Any imprecision comes only from rounding the answer.

What is 70 kg in pounds?
